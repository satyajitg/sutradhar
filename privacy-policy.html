Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 19, 2026
The short version: Sutradhar stores everything locally on your device. It does not collect, transmit, or share any personal data. No account is required. No ads. No tracking. If you record a lesson, the microphone (and, only if you turn it on, the camera) is used on your device to make the video, which is saved to your own Movies folder and never uploaded by the app. Pictures, PDF handouts and board files you make with Share leave your device only when you share, print or save them yourself.

🏠 1. Data Storage

All data created within Sutradhar — including boards, pages, drawings, shapes, text labels, and imported images — is stored exclusively on your device in the app's private storage directory. This data is never uploaded to any server.

Backups. If you choose "Back up all boards", the app writes a single backup file (a .zip of your boards and images) to the location you pick in Android's file picker, such as your Downloads folder, a USB stick, or a cloud storage app like Google Drive. The app never uploads the backup itself; if you pick a cloud location, that storage app handles it under its own terms. "Restore from backup" reads only the one file you pick, and adds its boards to the app's private storage.

Imported PDFs. If you choose "New board from PDF" or "Insert PDF slides", the app reads only the one PDF file you pick in Android's file picker. Each page is turned into a picture and stored in the app's private storage as part of your board. The original PDF is not modified, and nothing from it is uploaded or sent anywhere.

Lesson recordings. Recording is off unless you turn on "Lesson recording" in Settings. When you record a lesson, the video (your board and your voice, and your face if you turned on the camera button in the recording dock) is made on your device. It is saved to your device's Movies/Sutradhar folder, where your Gallery and other apps can see it (on Android 9 and older, to the app's own folder instead, which is removed with the app). The app never uploads a recording. It leaves your device only if you share it yourself, for example with the Share button, and then the app you share to handles it under its own terms.

When you uninstall the app, all data in the app's private storage is deleted by the operating system in accordance with standard Android behaviour. On Android 10 and newer, lesson videos saved to your Movies folder, and PDFs, pictures and board files you saved to your Downloads or Pictures folder, are yours and stay on the device until you delete them. On Android 9 and older, lesson videos are kept in the app's own folder instead, so they are removed with the app; share or copy any you want to keep before uninstalling.

📡 2. No Data Transmission

Sutradhar does not connect to the internet for any purpose. There are no analytics libraries, no crash-reporting SDKs, no advertising networks, and no cloud sync features. Your work never leaves your device unless you explicitly share, email, print or save it yourself (section 4), save a backup to a location you choose, or share a lesson video yourself. Audio and camera pictures are never sent anywhere by the app.

🔑 3. Permissions

The app asks for two permissions, and only for lesson recording:

  • Microphone, to record your voice in a lesson video. Android asks you only when you tap Record for the first time, and only if you have turned on "Lesson recording" in Settings. The sound goes straight into the video on your device. It is not recorded at any other time and not sent anywhere. If you mute the microphone with the button in the recording dock, the app still reads the microphone but records silence in its place, so the video keeps a continuous (silent) sound track; for this reason Android's microphone indicator stays on while you record, even when muted. While you are muted, the app checks only how loud the sound is, so it can tell you "You're muted" if you start talking. That check happens on your device only; the sound and its loudness are not kept, recorded or sent anywhere.
  • Camera, only if you tap the camera button in the recording dock. Android asks you the first time you turn it on. The front camera's pictures are used only for the small round picture of you in the video (and its preview on screen while you record). They are not saved separately, not analysed, and not sent anywhere. If you refuse, the lesson is recorded without your picture.

You can refuse either permission, or withdraw it later in Android's Settings, under Apps, Sutradhar, Permissions. Everything else in the app works without them. Recording stops when you leave the app; the app never uses the microphone or camera in the background.

Everything else uses Android features that don't need a permission:

  • Photo picker, used only when you tap the Image tool and choose a photo to place on the canvas. Android's picker gives the app just that one photo; the app can't browse your gallery. The photo is copied into the app's private storage, and the original is not modified.
  • Downloads and Pictures (MediaStore), used only when you tap "Save to Downloads" (a PDF or a board file) or "Save to Gallery" (a page picture, saved to Pictures/Sutradhar) in the Share sheet. This needs Android 10 or newer; on older versions these buttons aren't shown.
  • Android file picker (Storage Access Framework), used only when you back up or restore, import a PDF, or choose "Open board file". Android gives the app access to just the one file you choose to create or open; the app can't browse your other files.
  • Opening a board file from another app. When you tap a board file in an app such as WhatsApp, Gmail or Files and choose Sutradhar, Android gives the app that one file to read. Because some apps don't label files clearly, Android may offer Sutradhar for other files too; if you pick it, the app only checks whether the file is a board file, and adds nothing if it isn't.
  • Share sheet, clipboard and printing: see section 4.

No other files are written outside the app's private storage, apart from lesson videos saved to your Movies folder and files you save from the Share sheet. No location, contacts, storage or network permissions are requested.

📤 4. Sharing, Printing and the Clipboard

The Share button in a board makes a file on your device: a picture of the current page, a PDF handout, or a board file. The file is made in the app's private storage and goes nowhere until you choose what to do with it. The app clears these files out of its storage once they are a day old (the next time you share), and Android may clear them sooner.

  • Share and Email. The app hands the file to Android's share sheet or to your email app (Gmail if installed), and only to the app you pick. That app then handles it under its own terms. The app itself never sends anything.
  • Board files. A board file (.sxboard) contains one whole board: its pages, your writing and drawings, text labels, and the pictures and slides on it. Anyone you send it to can open and edit that board in Sutradhar, so share it only with people who should have it. When you open a board file someone sent you, its board is added to your boards on this device; nothing is replaced, and nothing is sent back.
  • Clipboard. The app puts something on the clipboard only when you tap "Copy": a picture of the current page, so you can paste it into another app. It never reads the clipboard. Like anything you copy, the picture stays on the clipboard until you copy something else, though the app tidies away its temporary copy of the picture after about a day, after which pasting it may no longer work. On Android 13 and newer, Android shows a confirmation when it's copied.
  • Pen diagnostics. While a board is open, the app keeps a short log of recent pen and finger movements (position, pressure, pen or finger) in memory only, to help fix writing problems. It contains no drawing content, no names and no account details, is never saved to storage on its own and is cleared when the app closes. It leaves the device only if you tap "Share pen diagnostics" in Settings and choose where to send it.
  • Printing. "Print" opens Android's own print screen with the PDF handout. Android's print system and the print service apps you have installed (for example, your printer maker's app) find the printer and send the pages to it, under their own terms; some print services may use your network or the internet to reach a printer. Sutradhar only passes the PDF to Android's print system and has no printer code of its own. "Save as PDF" in the print screen saves where you choose.

The app's "Sutradhar footer" on handouts shows the app's name and logo, the board's name and page numbers; you can turn it off. Nothing else about you or your device is added to pictures and PDFs. A board file also records the date and time it was made.

👶 5. Children's Privacy

Sutradhar does not knowingly collect any information from children under the age of 13. No data is collected from anyone: lesson recordings, handouts and board files stay on the device unless the person using it shares them.
